    How to read these plots
    • Each point is one sample. In the tissue panel, a grey line joins each patient's myometrium and leiomyoma.
    • The y axis is in TPM. On the log scale the spacing is logarithmic, which spreads out low values; the ticks still read in TPM. The axis always reaches at least 10 TPM, so a gene that is barely expressed looks that way instead of being stretched to fill the plot.
    • Mean TPM is the average over the samples in a group. The percentile says where that mean ranks among all protein-coding genes in the same group: the 50th is a typical gene, the 90th is in the top 10%.
    • TPM compares genes within a sample and samples within a dataset. The three datasets differ in material and library preparation, so values should not be compared across panels.
    • log2FC and padj come from DESeq2, fitted within each dataset. log2FC is the first group relative to the second, shrunken with apeglm so that noisy low-count genes are pulled toward zero. padj is the Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted p-value.
    • Brackets: * padj < 0.05, ** < 0.01, *** < 0.001, ns not significant. Hover a label for the exact value. "LM vs MM, all 10 pairs" pools both LM groups, so it has no bracket of its own.
    • "Not tested" means the gene had too few counts in that dataset for DESeq2 to test.
    • The spheroid wild-type group has only 2 replicates.
