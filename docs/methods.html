How the samples group

Each point is one sample, placed by the two directions along which its dataset varies most: DESeq2's PCA (plotPCA on variance-stabilized counts, the 500 most variable genes). Hover a point for the sample.

In the tissue, leiomyoma and myometrium separate on the first axis, and the MED12-mutant tumors sit furthest from the myometrium; the two wild-type tumors from patients 865 and 975 stand apart from the other two on the second axis. In the cells, replicates sit on top of each other. The knock-out is the most different 2D line, and in both formats the two mutant clones are as far from each other as from WT, so a change seen in only one clone may be a property of that clone rather than of the mutation.

What the values mean

TPM

Transcripts per million at the gene level (from Salmon). TPM corrects for sequencing depth and for gene length, so it compares genes with each other within a sample, and samples with each other within a dataset. It does not compare across the three datasets: tissue versus cultured cells, different library preparations and read lengths, and an unstranded 2D library.

Expression percentile

Where a gene's mean TPM in a group ranks among the 19,350 protein-coding genes with nonzero TPM in at least one sample. The 50th percentile is a typical gene; the 90th is in the top 10%.

log2FC and padj

From DESeq2, fitted separately in each dataset. log2FC is the first group relative to the second, so a positive value means higher in the first group. The site shows fold changes with noisy estimates from low-count genes pulled toward zero (shrinkage with apeglm); the downloadable tables keep both the shrunken (log2FC_shrunk) and the raw (log2FoldChange) values. padj is the p-value adjusted for the number of genes tested (Benjamini–Hochberg). A gene was tested only if it had at least 10 counts in at least as many samples as the smallest group.

The patient term pairs each leiomyoma with its own myometrium, the multi-gene analog of a paired t-test. Because every patient contributes a myometrium baseline, LM mut vs LM WT asks whether the leiomyoma-versus-myometrium change differs between MED12-mutant and wild-type tumors, although the two kinds of tumor come from different patients.

Caveats: the tissue comparisons by genotype use 6 mutant and 4 wild-type patients; the spheroid WT group has 2 replicates. No batch information (preparation or sequencing run) is recorded in GEO or SRA for any dataset. DESeq2 1.50.2, apeglm 1.32.0, R 4.5.2.

Processing

Reads

Runs were downloaded from NCBI SRA's AWS mirror and converted to FASTQ with sra-tools 3.0.3 (vdb-validate, then fasterq-dump --split-3). Every file was checked against the run's read count in SRA.

Reference

GRCh38 primary assembly with the GENCODE v44 comprehensive annotation (62,298 genes), the same GENCODE release as 10x Genomics' 2024-A reference but unfiltered.

Quantification

nf-core/rnaseq 3.27.0 (Nextflow 26.04.3, Docker), in Salmon-only mode: no genome alignment.

  • Quality control and trimming: FastQC 0.12.1, fastp 1.3.6.
  • Strandedness inferred per sample from a subsample of reads, then passed to Salmon.
  • Salmon 2.7.0 against a decoy-aware index: GENCODE v44 transcripts plus the whole genome as decoys, k = 31.
  • Transcript estimates summarized to genes with tximport (tximeta 1.20.1).

Reproducing

Every step is a script in github.com/brandonlukas/med12-tpm. Needs Nextflow ≥ 25.10.4, Docker, sra-tools, pigz, R with DESeq2 and apeglm, and Python with pandas.

./fetch_reference.sh            # GENCODE v44 fasta + GTF, md5-checked
./download_fastqs.sh 4          # 40 runs from SRA, read counts checked
./run_rnaseq.sh                 # nf-core/rnaseq (about 2 h on 44 cores)
python3 split_tpm.py            # per-dataset TPM tables
Rscript deseq2.R                # DESeq2 tables
python3 build_site_data.py      # data for this site
